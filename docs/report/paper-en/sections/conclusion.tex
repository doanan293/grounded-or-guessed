\section{Conclusion and Future Work}
\label{sec:conclusion}

SemLoc's results reproduce, but its top-1 accuracy is mostly the LLM's own guess, boosted by an undocumented bonus;
execution evidence earns its keep at top three. Handing that evidence to the model directly---one prompt with per-line
coverage---is more accurate, about five times cheaper and robust to renaming. Evaluation choices and benchmark labels
move the numbers as much as methods do. We suggest that every evaluation of LLM-based fault localization (i) breaks ties
by average rank with a fixed denominator, (ii) verifies its fault locations by execution, (iii) ablates every component,
including undocumented ones, (iv) repeats runs and reports confidence intervals, (v) reports cost, and (vi) compares
against a single prompt with the same evidence. Future work includes a multi-location oracle that rejects compensating
fixes, real bugs from more projects, and function-level localization.
