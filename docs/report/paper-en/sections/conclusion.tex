\section{Conclusion and Future Work}
\label{sec:conclusion}

SemLoc's published numbers recompute exactly from its logs, but its top-1 answer is, by an undocumented design choice,
the LLM's own guess; its execution evidence earns its keep at top three. On faults that a failing test reaches, handing
execution evidence to the model directly---one prompt with the failing tests and per-line coverage---is more accurate,
about five times cheaper and shows no detectable change under renaming; it cannot find faults no test reaches. These
results come from one main model, one benchmark with repaired labels and 31 real bugs from one project. Evaluation choices
and benchmark labels move the numbers as much as methods do, so we suggest that every evaluation of LLM-based fault
localization apply the seven checks of Table~\ref{tab:checklist}. Future work includes a multi-location oracle that
rejects compensating fixes, an ablation separating coverage from test outcomes in the prompt, real bugs from more
projects, and function-level localization.
