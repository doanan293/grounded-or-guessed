\section{Related Work and Research Direction}
\label{sec:related}

\textbf{Spectrum- and mutation-based localization.} Tarantula~\cite{jones2002}, Ochiai~\cite{abreu2009} and
DStar~\cite{wong2014dstar} score statements from coverage; surveys~\cite{wong2016survey,desouza2016} note two evaluation
pitfalls we revisit: tie handling and omission faults, which have no faulty line to blame. Mutation-based
localization~\cite{papadakis2015,moon2014} replaces coverage with mutant behavior; delta
debugging~\cite{zeller2002tse} and cause-effect chains~\cite{zeller2002chains} underlie counterfactual reasoning; and
Daikon~\cite{ernst2007daikon} infers likely invariants from traces, whereas SemLoc asks an LLM for them.

\textbf{LLM-based localization.} AutoFL~\cite{kang2024autofl} lets an LLM navigate code with tool calls at method level;
AgentFL~\cite{qin2025agentfl} scales this to projects with agents; FlexFL~\cite{xu2025flexfl} feeds SBFL output to
open-source LLMs, the closest idea to our coverage baseline but at method level on Java. An industrial study of crash
reports~\cite{medeiros2026crash} finds that mined rankings in the prompt help LLMs localize, and EffiHolmes~\cite{yang2026effiholmes}
contrasts two executions (normal vs.\ scaled workload) before LLM reasoning for performance faults. SemLoc~\cite{yang2026semloc}
is the first to rank lines by violations of LLM-inferred executable constraints. Wu et al.~\cite{wu2023llmfl} find that
error logs markedly improve ChatGPT's localization, which works at function level but degrades at class level. None of
these works measures how much of the accuracy comes from execution evidence rather than from the model's own guess, or
compares with the plain practice of handing the failing tests to the model. For repository-level repair,
Agentless~\cite{xia2024agentless} shows that a simple two-phase process beats more elaborate agents at lower cost.

\textbf{Practice.} Surveys report broad use of AI assistants by developers~\cite{stackoverflow2025,jetbrains2025} and
DevGPT~\cite{xiao2024devgpt} collects real developer--ChatGPT conversations, debugging among them. Parnin and
Orso~\cite{parnin2011} showed that automated debugging rests on assumptions that do not match how developers debug, and
Kochhar et al.~\cite{kochhar2016} surveyed practitioners' expectations of fault localization tools.

\textbf{Benchmarks and validity.} Defects4J~\cite{just2014}, BugsInPy~\cite{widyasari2020} and QuixBugs~\cite{lin2017quixbugs}
provide faults with triggering tests. Pearson et al.~\cite{pearson2017} show that results on artificial faults do not
transfer to real ones and that tie and omission handling can reorder techniques. For LLMs, threats include closed-model
drift and benchmark contamination~\cite{sallou2024,ramos2024memorize,xu2024contam}, mitigated by rewriting or renaming.
Label errors are common even in flagship benchmarks and can reorder models~\cite{northcutt2021labels}; Agentless also
filters problematic SWE-bench tasks~\cite{xia2024agentless}.

\textbf{Plausible patches.} In program repair, a patch that passes all tests is only \emph{plausible}: most reported
patches of early generate-and-validate systems were incorrect~\cite{qi2015kali}, and repair overfits weak test
suites~\cite{smith2015cure}. We meet the same effect from the localization side.

\textbf{Direction.} We combine these threads: we audit scoring as Pearson et al.\ recommend, check the benchmark's labels,
test memorization by renaming, measure the share of accuracy that is grounded, and make the developers' plain practice
the baseline every pipeline must beat.
